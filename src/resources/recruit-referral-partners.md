To recruit referral partners for a B2B tool, go to the people a client already
pays to run their outbound: done-for-you outbound agencies, fractional sales
leaders, and the consultants who build sales workflows for clients. They decide
which tools a client uses and they are judged on meetings booked. Pay them one
flat share of what a referred client pays, and write to them about their
clients' open problem before you mention the money, because the money is the
smallest reason they will say yes.

How to find the agencies is covered in [how to find agencies that will resell
your product](/resources/find-agencies-to-resell-you/), and why an agency is
worth more than a direct customer is in [why one agency is worth three
customers](/resources/selling-to-agencies/). This page is the channel itself:
who to recruit, what to pay them and what to write.

## Recruit the people already paid to run a client's outbound

A referral partner is somebody who makes most of their money from consulting
and sends you clients on the side. That is what makes the channel cheap: you
pay when a client pays, and the partner has an income whether or not they ever
send you anyone.

The partners worth having share one fact. Their client pays them to produce
pipeline, so they pick the tools the client runs on and they are measured on
the meetings those tools produce. A referral from them arrives with the tool
already chosen, because the partner chose it for their own work.

This is a different arrangement from the commission-only salesperson that
[do not delegate sales early](/resources/do-not-delegate-sales/) warns against.
That person has no income until they close. A referral partner has a practice,
and the referral rides on work they do anyway.

## Rank partner types by why their clients need you

Rank the types by why their clients would need what you sell, never by how
many of each type exist. A partner whose client has no use for your tool will
never send one, however many of them there are.

- Done-for-you outbound agencies come first. They write every client's cold
  email on a monthly retainer, so a tool that does the writing replaces hours
  the agency pays for. An agency can run it for its own clients at a partner
  discount, or send you a client for a referral fee, or both.
- Fractional sales leaders own a founder's pipeline for three to twelve months.
  They pick the tools and they are judged on meetings. A tool that hands them a
  brief and drafts on day one is one they will choose.
- Consultants who build AI and automation workflows for clients can bill the
  setup of your tool inside a client's workflow as a deliverable. They earn a
  setup fee from the client and a referral from you.
- Revenue operations and CRM implementers set up a client's CRM and leave it
  empty, so a tool that decides what goes in fits their package. They are slow:
  they expect a marketplace listing and deal registration, and they already
  earn a share from the CRM vendors they resell.
- Founder-led-sales coaches sell the advice itself. A coach whose practice caps
  how many clients it takes refers the overflow. Recruit them one at a time;
  they are a handful of referrals, never a channel.
- White-label resellers keep the setup fee and most of the recurring revenue.
  That arrangement waits until customers have renewed and you know what you
  are giving away.

Most of these people list themselves in the tool vendors' own partner
directories, which is where the search for the agencies starts too.

## Pay one flat number and keep it flat

Comparable referral programmes pay a partner 20% to 35% of a referred
customer's payments, most of them for twelve months and some for as long as
the customer stays. A published benchmark across 250 SaaS programmes averages
22% to 25%. Pick one number inside that range and keep it flat.

A worked example: pay 20% of what a referred client pays for their first
twelve months, hold each payout thirty days, and make the same 20% the discount
a partner gets on workspaces they pay for themselves. One number, used both
ways, with no cash bounty per signed client. A rising tier
can wait until one partner has five paying clients and asks for it.

## The commission is a tip

Work the arithmetic before you lead with the money. At $999 a month, 20% is
about $200 a month, about $2,400 over the twelve months, or about $2,000 once
if the client pays $9,990 for the year up front. A fractional sales leader bills
a client many times that in a month. Against that fee the referral is a tip.

So the commission gets a partner to answer, and something else gets them to
send a client. What a partner buys is the deliverable: the brief and the drafts
their client would otherwise wait on them for, which they can now bill a setup
fee for or stop producing by hand. Write the offer around that, and treat the
share as the detail it is.

## Write the money last

The message to a consultant has three parts, in this order: their stake, which
is their clients' open problem; your claim, which is what the tool does for
that client; and the money. One ask, one price, no feature list. The subject
names what the partner sells, which is work inside their client's business,
never what you sell.

> **Subject:** A tool to set up inside your clients' sales workflow
>
> Hi {first name},
>
> Your clients still pay someone to pick which companies to approach and to
> write what goes out. {Your tool} does both. {One sentence on what it does
> today, inside the workflow you already build for them.} It costs {your price}.
>
> Set it up for a client and bill the setup the way you do now. Send us a
> client who pays us directly and we pay you {the share} of what they pay for
> their first twelve months.
>
> Worth a 15-minute call?

The first sentence belongs to the reader's client. The second is the claim, and
it promises only what the tool does today. The money is one sentence at the end,
after the sentence that tells the consultant how they bill for it.

## Start with the partners you already know

Send in this order. First, the partners of your own kind, which for most B2B
tools means the outbound agencies you have already found and ranked, each with
a fact from its own page in the second sentence. Second, the consultants, with
the message above. Third, fractional sales leaders, through two or three of the
communities where they gather, since no directory lists them the way the
vendors list agencies.

Skip for now the CRM implementers, who wait for a marketplace listing, the
coaches as a channel, and white label. Have the share working before the first
message goes, with a way to tell which client a partner sent you. For the first
handful of partners a spreadsheet kept by hand is enough.

## Five things

1. Recruit the people a client already pays to run their outbound, because they
   choose the tools and are judged on meetings.
2. Rank partner types by why their clients need you: agencies, then fractional
   sales leaders, then workflow consultants, with implementers, coaches and
   resellers later.
3. Pay one flat share of a referred client's payments, and add a tier only when
   a partner with five paying clients asks.
4. Lead with the deliverable the partner can bill for, because at a consultant's
   fees the commission is a tip.
5. Write the message in the order stake, claim, money, with a subject that names
   what the partner sells.
